faqs:
  - q: Do I need a permit to remodel a bathroom in Bradenton?
    a: >-
      Yes, for anything that moves plumbing or electrical. We're not a general
      contractor, so we don't pull permits ourselves – the licensed general
      contractor we work with files it. Inside city limits that's the City of
      Bradenton Building Department; in unincorporated Bradenton it's Manatee
      County Building & Development Services. Review typically takes 2–4
      business days. A cosmetic swap that keeps every fixture in place usually
      doesn't need one.
  - q: What does a bathroom remodel cost in Bradenton?
    a: >-
      Most Bradenton bathrooms land between $18,000 and $45,000. A
      same-footprint hall bath runs $18,000–$26,000; a new layout with a
      walk-in shower and frameless glass runs $26,000–$38,000; a primary suite
      with a wet room starts around $38,000. Those ranges cover demo,
      waterproofing, tile, glass and haul-away; any plumbing, electrical, or
      permit work is quoted separately by our general contractor partner.
  - q: How long does a Bradenton bathroom remodel take?
    a: >-
      Budget four to six weeks for a standard hall bath and six to eight for a
      primary suite. If a permit is needed, review is 2–4 business days; demo
      and any rough-in by the licensed trades run about a week, waterproofing adds a 24-hour flood test, tile and finishes
      take two to three weeks, and frameless glass adds 7–10 days because it
      can't be measured until the tile is set. The Bayshore Gardens job on this
      page ran 24 working days.
  - q: Can we stay in the house while you work?
    a: >-
      Almost always, as long as you have a second bathroom. We seal the doorway
      with a zippered barrier, run negative-air dust control, protect the path
      in and out, and clean the work area every afternoon. Water is only shut
      off for a few hours on the plumber's days, and we tell you which days
      those are at the start. If the bathroom we're remodeling is your only one, say
      so early and we'll build the schedule around it.
  - q: Can the toilet or layout move on a slab home?
    a: >-
      Usually yes, but we don't do plumbing ourselves – we bring in the general
      contractor we work with, or recommend a licensed plumber. Most Bradenton
      homes are slab-on-grade, so a new toilet or shower location means
      core-drilling or trenching the slab for the drain line. It's priced as a
      separate line so you can decide whether the new layout is worth it before
      anything is cut.
  - q: My HOA or condo association needs approval. Can you handle that?
    a: >-
      We'll give you the packet most Bradenton associations ask for – scope of
      work, our liability insurance certificate, working hours, and the
      elevator or stairwell protection plan. River Strand, Perico Bay, and most
      Longboat Key buildings also cap work hours and require a certificate
      naming the association as additional insured; we issue that at no charge.
      You submit it; we've never had one rejected.
  - q: What happens if you find rot or a leak behind the old tile?
    a: >-
      You get a photo of it and a price for the repair before we carry on.
      Bradenton baths built before the mid-90s often hide a failed mortar bed or
      a supply line that's been weeping for years, and it's the single most
      common surprise we find. We cut back to sound framing and replace the
      sheathing, and if the source is a pipe, a licensed plumber fixes it first –
      tiling over it only hides it for a season.
