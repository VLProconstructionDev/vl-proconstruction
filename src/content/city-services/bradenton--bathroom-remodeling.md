faqs:
  - q: Do I need a permit to remodel a bathroom in Bradenton?
    a: >-
      Yes, for anything that moves plumbing or electrical – which a full remodel
      always does. We pull it. Inside city limits that's the City of Bradenton
      Building Department; in unincorporated Bradenton it's Manatee County
      Building & Development Services. Review typically takes 2–4 business days,
      and we schedule the rough-in inspection so it never adds a day to the
      build. A cosmetic swap that keeps every fixture in place usually doesn't
      need one.
  - q: What does a bathroom remodel cost in Bradenton?
    a: >-
      Most Bradenton bathrooms land between $18,000 and $45,000. A
      same-footprint hall bath runs $18,000–$26,000; a full remodel with a new
      layout, relocated plumbing, and frameless glass runs $26,000–$38,000; a
      primary suite with a wet room starts around $38,000. Those ranges include
      demo, waterproofing, tile, vanity and fixtures, permit fees, and
      haul-away.
  - q: How long does a Bradenton bathroom remodel take?
    a: >-
      Budget four to six weeks for a standard hall bath and six to eight for a
      primary suite. Permitting is 2–4 business days, demo and rough-in run
      about a week, waterproofing adds a 24-hour flood test, tile and finishes
      take two to three weeks, and frameless glass adds 7–10 days because it
      can't be measured until the tile is set. The Bayshore Gardens job on this
      page ran 24 working days.
  - q: Can we stay in the house while you work?
    a: >-
      Almost always, as long as you have a second bathroom. We seal the doorway
      with a zippered barrier, run negative-air dust control, protect the path
      in and out, and clean the work area every afternoon. Water is only shut
      off for a few hours on the plumbing days, and we tell you which days those
      are at the start. If the bathroom we're remodeling is your only one, say
      so early and we'll build the schedule around it.
  - q: Can you move the toilet or change the layout on a slab home?
    a: >-
      Usually yes. Most Bradenton homes are slab-on-grade, so a new toilet or
      shower location means core-drilling or trenching the slab for the drain
      line rather than working from below. It's a day or two of concrete work
      plus the patch and the inspection, and we price it as a line item so you
      can decide whether the new layout is worth it before anything is cut.
  - q: My HOA or condo association needs approval. Can you handle that?
    a: >-
      We'll give you the packet most Bradenton associations ask for – scope of
      work, our license and liability certificate, working hours, and the
      elevator or stairwell protection plan. River Strand, Perico Bay, and most
      Longboat Key buildings also cap work hours and require a certificate
      naming the association as additional insured; we issue that at no charge.
      You submit it; we've never had one rejected.
  - q: What happens if you find rot or a leak behind the old tile?
    a: >-
      You get a photo of it and a price for the repair before we carry on.
      Bradenton baths built before the mid-90s often hide a failed mortar bed or
      a supply line that's been weeping for years, and it's the single most
      common surprise we find. We cut back to sound framing, replace the
      sheathing, and fix the source – tiling over it only hides it for a season.
