faqs:
  - q: Can you build a curbless shower on a Bradenton slab-on-grade home?
    a: >-
      Almost always, and most of the ones we build here are on slab. There are
      two ways to get the fall without a curb — saw-cut and recess the shower
      footprint, or build an engineered sloped assembly and bring the bathroom
      floor up to meet it. Which one fits depends on your drain location, the
      slab depth, and how the doorway threshold lands. We read the slab and
      camera the line on the first visit and tell you then whether a fully flush
      entry works or whether a low 1/2" lip is the smarter call.
  - q: Will a curbless shower flood the bathroom floor?
    a: >-
      Not when it's designed right. Three things control the water — enough fall
      to the drain across the whole pan, a drain sized and placed for the flow,
      and glass or a wall positioned to catch spray. A linear drain at the back
      wall lets the entire pan slope one way, which is why we use one on most
      curbless builds. With a recessed threshold strip the entry stays dry
      without a curb at all.
  - q: What's the difference between a walk-in shower and a curbless shower?
    a: >-
      A walk-in shower is any shower you step into instead of over a tub wall —
      most have a low tiled curb, usually 3 to 5 inches. A curbless shower has
      no curb at all, so the bathroom floor runs straight in. Every curbless
      shower is a walk-in; not every walk-in is curbless. Curbless costs more
      because of the slab or framing work, and it's what you want for wheelchair
      access, a walker or rollator, or a room that reads as one continuous
      floor.
  - q: What does a curbless walk-in shower cost in Bradenton?
    a: >-
      Most Bradenton walk-in showers land between $9,500 and $22,000. A low-curb
      walk-in in the same footprint runs $9,500–$14,000; a curbless
      zero-threshold build with a linear drain and large-format tile runs
      $14,000–$22,000; a full roll-in wet room starts around $22,000. Those
      ranges include demo, slab work, waterproofing, tile setting, permit fees,
      and haul-away.
  - q: Do I need a permit for a walk-in shower in Bradenton or Manatee County?
    a: >-
      If the shower stays exactly where it is and nothing moves, usually not.
      Converting a tub to a walk-in, adding or relocating a drain, cutting the
      slab, or altering a wall does need one — and a curbless build almost
      always touches at least one of those. We pull it as the licensed
      contractor of record: City of Bradenton Building Department inside city
      limits, Manatee County Building & Development Services outside them.
      Review typically takes 2–4 business days, and the inspection is scheduled
      around our flood test so it never adds a day to your build.
  - q: How long does a curbless shower take start to finish?
    a: >-
      A standard walk-in shower in the existing footprint runs about 2 to 3
      weeks. A curbless build runs 3 to 4 weeks, because the slab work and the
      24-hour flood test both have to happen before tile. Frameless glass adds 2
      to 3 weeks after the tile is finished — it's templated off the finished
      surfaces, so it can't be ordered in advance. The Palma Sola job on this
      page ran 13 working days of build time.
  - q: How wide does the entry need to be for a wheelchair?
    a: >-
      For a fully accessible roll-in shower, ADA guidance is a 60" x 30" minimum
      interior and a 36" clear opening, with no threshold over 1/2" and that
      half-inch beveled. Most Bradenton homeowners aren't building to full ADA —
      they want a shower that works now and still works later — so we size the
      opening as wide as the room allows and set real blocking in the walls for
      grab bars, whether or not the bars go in today.
  - q: Do you offer a warranty on the waterproofing?
    a: >-
      Yes. Our labor is warranted for two years and the Schluter®-KERDI system

      membrane is installed to their written spec and flood-tested for 24 hours.
