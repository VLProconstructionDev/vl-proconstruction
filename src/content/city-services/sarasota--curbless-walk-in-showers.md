faqs:
  - q: Can you build a curbless shower in a mid-century Sarasota home?
    a: >-
      Usually — but we scope the drain first. Gulf Gate and Arlington Park homes
      from the 1950s and 60s often have cast-iron waste lines and terrazzo
      floors, so we camera the line and read the slab before promising a flush
      entry. When the slab allows it we cut only the pan opening and build the
      slope into the pan, which leaves the terrazzo outside the bathroom intact.
      When it doesn't, we'll tell you at the first visit that a low 1/2" lip is
      the smarter call.
  - q: Can a curbless shower be built in a Sarasota condo?
    a: >-
      Yes, and we build them downtown and on the keys — but the fall comes from
      a built-up sloped assembly rather than a cut deck, because above the
      ground floor the structural slab usually can't be trenched. That raises
      the shower floor slightly, so the bathroom floor is brought up to meet it
      and the entry stays flush. The association's sound-rating mat goes in
      under the membrane, and we handle the packet, the posted work hours, the
      elevator booking, and the scheduled water shutoff.
  - q: Will a curbless shower flood the bathroom floor?
    a: >-
      Not when it's designed right. Three things control the water — enough fall
      to the drain across the whole pan, a drain sized and placed for the flow,
      and glass or a wall positioned to catch spray. A linear drain at the back
      wall lets the entire pan slope one way, which is why we use one on most
      curbless builds. With a recessed threshold strip the entry stays dry
      without a curb at all.
  - q: What does a curbless walk-in shower cost in Sarasota?
    a: >-
      Most Sarasota walk-in showers land between $11,000 and $26,000. A low-curb
      walk-in in the same footprint runs $11,000–$15,000; a curbless
      zero-threshold build with a linear drain and large-format tile runs
      $15,000–$26,000; a full roll-in wet room starts around $26,000. Those
      ranges include demo, slab work, waterproofing, tile setting, permit fees,
      and haul-away.
  - q: Do I need a permit for a walk-in shower in Sarasota?
    a: >-
      If the shower stays exactly where it is and nothing moves, usually not.
      Converting a tub to a walk-in, adding or relocating a drain, cutting the
      slab, or altering a wall does need one — and a curbless build almost
      always touches at least one of those. We pull it as the licensed
      contractor of record: City of Sarasota Development Services inside city
      limits, Sarasota County Building Services in Gulf Gate, Siesta Key, Palmer
      Ranch, and the rest of the unincorporated county. Review typically takes
      3–5 business days.
  - q: How long does a curbless shower take start to finish?
    a: >-
      A standard walk-in shower in the existing footprint runs about 2 to 3
      weeks. A curbless build runs 3 to 4 weeks, because the slab work and the
      24-hour flood test both have to happen before tile. Frameless glass adds 2
      to 3 weeks after the tile is finished — it's templated off the finished
      surfaces, so it can't be ordered in advance. The Gulf Gate job on this
      page ran 12 working days of build time, and condo approvals can add a week
      or two on the front end.
  - q: How wide does the entry need to be for a wheelchair?
    a: >-
      For a fully accessible roll-in shower, ADA guidance is a 60" x 30" minimum
      interior and a 36" clear opening, with no threshold over 1/2" and that
      half-inch beveled. Most Sarasota homeowners aren't building to full ADA —
      they want a shower that works now and still works later — so we size the
      opening as wide as the room allows and set real blocking in the walls for
      grab bars, whether or not the bars go in today.
  - q: Do you offer a warranty on the waterproofing?
    a: >-
      Yes. Our labor is warranted for two years and the Schluter®-KERDI system

      membrane is installed to their written spec and flood-tested for 24 hours.
      You get the flood-test photos and the product documentation with your
      final invoice.
