## Sarasota flood zones and the FEMA 50% rule

Three things decide what a Sarasota bathroom costs, and none of them are tile. The first is the flood map. A remodel that would be routine in Lakewood Ranch can trigger a full structural compliance requirement on Siesta Key, and the threshold that decides it is calculated differently depending on which side of a municipal line your house sits.

If your home is in a special flood hazard area, any improvement worth 50% or more of the structure's market value triggers full compliance with current flood regulations — elevation, flood-resistant materials, flood venting. That is no longer a bathroom budget.

What changes, and this is the part nobody writes down, is whether your jurisdiction counts that per project or adds projects up over a rolling window. Same metro, different answers:

| Jurisdiction | Cumulative period | What that means for your project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| City of Sarasota | <span class="row-us">Per project</span> | Bird Key, Lido, St. Armands. Each permit is assessed on its own. The city maintains a Substantial Improvement Package for the paperwork. |
| Sarasota County (unincorporated) | <span class="row-us">No multi-year window</span> | Siesta Key, Casey Key, Gulf Gate. Market value is the appraiser's building-only value plus 20%, or a certified private appraisal. Near-simultaneous permits are combined — you cannot phase around it. |
| Town of Longboat Key | <span class="row-us">10 years cumulative</span> | The strictest in the region. Improvements add up over a rolling decade, so work you did years ago still counts against the threshold. |
| City of Venice | <span class="row-us">1 year cumulative</span> | Improvements permitted within the same 12-month window are totalled together. |
| City of North Port | <span class="row-us">Per project</span> | Cumulative language was removed in August 2024, so each permit now stands alone. |
| Manatee County | <span class="row-us">1 year cumulative</span> | Permits pulled inside a 12-month window are added together for the threshold. |

## Bathroom remodel permits in Sarasota

Inside city limits you are dealing with City of Sarasota Development Services. In Gulf Gate, Siesta Key, Palmer Ranch and the rest of unincorporated Sarasota County it is the county's Building Services division. Longboat Key, Venice and North Port each run their own. We file as the licensed contractor of record and meet the inspector. The same permitting applies to a standalone [custom shower build in Sarasota](/services/custom-showers/sarasota-fl/).

The city's rule is broad: a permit is required to erect, install, enlarge, alter, repair, remove, convert or replace any electrical, gas, mechanical or plumbing system. The exemptions are narrower than people expect — stopping a leak, clearing a stoppage, and removing and reinstalling a water closet, so long as the work doesn't replace valves, pipes or fixtures. Swapping a vanity with new supply lines is not exempt.

Two inspections shape the schedule. Plumbing rough-in has to pass before walls close, and there is a final at the end. Plan review runs 3–5 business days at the county and longer in the city, and the wait between calling an inspection and getting one is the county's calendar, not ours. We build it into the timeline instead of discovering it.

## Remodeling a condo bathroom in Sarasota

The second thing that decides the job is the building. In a condo, the board schedule, the service elevator and the riser shut-off set your start date long before the county does. About 27% of this county's housing is attached or multifamily, and it is the segment every competitor page ignores. Board or architectural approval comes on top of the municipal permit, never instead of it.

What buildings here typically want before you start:

- A written scope of work, sometimes with drawings
- Our license and a liability certificate naming the association as additional insured
- Posted work hours, and a corridor and elevator protection plan
- A booked service elevator and a scheduled riser shut-off
- Proof the sound-control assembly meets the building's rating

Season matters too. Many buildings restrict renovation work from January through April, which is exactly when the trade calendar is fullest. Summer opens up but runs into storm season. If you are north for the summer, that is the easiest job we do — we work an empty house, send photos at each milestone, and you come back to a finished bathroom. And if your board has just levied a milestone-inspection or SIRS assessment, say so early: we would rather scope to the budget you actually have than hand you a quote you have to walk away from.

## Older Sarasota homes: what we find behind the wall

The third is the slab. The median Sarasota County home was built in 1988, and 37.8% of the county's entire housing stock went up between 1970 and 1989. That cohort is consistent about what it hides: cast-iron waste lines scaled down to half their bore, galvanized supply that has been closing up for forty years, and plumbing buried in the slab in a position the drawings disagree with.

None of that is a reason to panic, and all of it is a reason to look first. We camera the drain and read the slab at the quote visit, so the surprise happens before you have signed rather than after the tub is out. Where the waste line has to be replaced we price the run to the stack as part of the job rather than as a discovery, and the [subfloor and waterproofing work](/services/waterproofing-subfloor-preparation/sarasota-fl/) is quoted with it rather than after it.

Permit fees are a line on your quote, not a surprise at the end. So is the change order: if we open a wall and find something, you get a photo of it and a price for the difference before we carry on.
