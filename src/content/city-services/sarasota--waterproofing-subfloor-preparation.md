## What the code and the standards actually require

Most of waterproofing is national — membranes, mortars and slope don't change at the county line. So rather than dress it up as local, here is the set of numbers the work is built to. Ask any contractor bidding your shower which of these they are installing to; the answer tells you most of what you need.

| Requirement | Standard | What it means on your job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shower pan test | <span class="row-us">IRC P2503.6</span> | The liner is filled to not less than 2 inches above the rough threshold and must hold for 15 minutes, before the walls close. Carried into the Florida Residential Code and signed off by the inspector. |
| Shower floor slope | <span class="row-us">1/4"–1/2" per foot</span> | Minimum quarter inch, maximum half inch per foot to the drain. The pre-slope under the liner is the step most failures skipped. |
| Waterproof membrane | <span class="row-us">ANSI A118.10</span> | The standard a membrane has to meet to be called waterproof. If a product doesn't cite it, it isn't one. |
| Shower receptor assembly | <span class="row-us">TCNA B415</span> | Requires an A118.10 membrane or vapor retarder on the walls, not just the pan. Walls are not optional. |
| Movement joints | <span class="row-us">TCNA EJ171</span> | Where soft joints go at perimeters, changes of plane and over substrate joints. Omitted joints are a leading cause of tented tile. |
| Setting mortars | <span class="row-us">ANSI A118.4 / .11 / .15</span> | Modified thin-set, EGP for plywood, improved modified. The mortar is specified to the substrate, not chosen by what's on the truck. |

## The waterproofing system

A continuous bonded membrane across pan, curb and walls, tied in at every corner, seam and pipe penetration, with the drain flange bonded to the sheet rather than caulked to it. Pre-slope built in before the membrane, never after — that single sequence is the difference between a pan that drains and a pan that holds water against the liner for twenty years.

Movement joints get laid out before the first tile is cut, per TCNA EJ171 — part of how we set <a href="/services/tile-natural-stone/sarasota-fl/">tile and natural stone</a> on every job. A large share of what gets called a waterproofing failure is tented or cracked tile from joints that were never installed. They cost nothing at setting time and are unfixable afterwards without taking the floor up.

## The pan test

The pan is plugged, filled to not less than two inches above the rough threshold, and held. Tile goes on after the water level has not moved — not before, and not on a promise. We photograph the level at the start and at the end of the hold, the inspector signs it, and both images go to you with the final invoice.

If the level drops, we find out why while everything is still open and nothing has been covered up. That is the entire reason the test exists, and the reason it happens before the walls close rather than after.

## Curbless and zero-entry showers

38.8% of Sarasota County residents are 65 or older, which makes zero-entry the fastest-growing request in this market and the easiest one to get wrong.

With no curb to stop it, the waterproofing plane has to run continuously past the opening, and the slope has to be built into the structure rather than floated on top of it. On a slab that usually means cutting and recessing; on a wood subfloor it means sistering and dropping the joist bay. Neither is a detail you can add later, which is why a curbless shower is priced from what is under the floor rather than from its square footage.

Done properly it reads as a well-designed bathroom rather than an institutional one — no threshold, no lip, no step to negotiate at six in the morning. See how we build them on the <a href="/services/custom-showers/sarasota-fl/">Sarasota custom shower page</a>.

## Signs your shower has already failed

- Loose or crumbling grout at the base, coming back within months of a repair
- A musty smell that doesn't clear with ventilation
- A stain on the ceiling of the room below
- Tile that has tented or cracked in a line
- A soft or spongy spot at the curb or threshold

Any one of those is worth opening up. None of them are fixed by re-grouting or re-sealing the surface — grout is the wear layer, not the waterproofing, and sealing a porous surface does not make it a membrane.
