faqs:
  - q: Is sealed grout enough to keep a shower from leaking?
    a: No. Grout and tile are the wear layer, not the waterproofing — both are porous, and every shower assembly is expected to get wet behind the tile. What keeps water out of the framing is a continuous bonded membrane underneath, sealed at the corners, seams and drain. If that layer is missing, no amount of sealer or caulk will save it.
  - q: What is a flood test?
    a: We plug the drain, fill the finished shower pan with water and leave it for 24 hours, marking the level at the start and checking it at the end. If the level holds and nothing shows below, the assembly is sound. It is the only way to know a pan is watertight before tile buries it, and it happens on every shower we build.
  - q: How flat does my subfloor need to be?
    a: For large-format tile the industry standard is 1/8" in 10 feet, which is far flatter than most Florida slabs come out of the ground. We measure the room first, and if it misses, we flatten it with patch or self-leveling underlayment. Setting large tile on a wavy substrate is what produces lippage — the edges you catch with a bare foot.
  - q: Can you waterproof a shower if another contractor is setting the tile?
    a: Yes. We take waterproofing and subfloor prep as standalone work, hand over the flood-test photos and the manufacturer documentation, and the tile setter picks it up from there. Our workmanship warranty covers the assembly we built.
  - q: What happens if you find a rotted subfloor?
    a: You get a photo of it and a price for the repair before we carry on. Rot means cutting back to sound framing, replacing the sheathing, and finding out where the water came from — a failed pan, a leaking supply line, or a toilet flange that has been weeping for years. Tiling over it only hides it for a season.
  - q: Do you test slab moisture before a floor goes down?
    a: On slab-on-grade, yes. Hardwood, LVP and most adhesives have a moisture limit, and a slab here can read high years after the pour. We run in-situ relative humidity probes per ASTM F2170, and if the number is over the limit we specify a moisture-mitigation coating rather than let the floor fail in year two.
