Somewhere between the third time you stepped over a tub wall you never use and the first
time a family member said "be careful getting out of there," the idea shows up: get rid of
the tub, put in a real walk-in shower.

It is the single most requested bathroom change we see across Bradenton, Lakewood Ranch,
and Longboat Key — and for good reason. In a market where a lot of homes were built with
one guest tub nobody bathes in and a primary bath that eats floor space, converting a tub
to a shower buys you daily comfort now and lets you stay in the house comfortably for
decades.

Here's the practical version: what a tub to shower conversion actually involves, what it
costs, when you need a permit in Manatee and Sarasota County, and how to tell a shower
built to last from one that will leak in five years.

## Why Gulf Coast homeowners are pulling the tub

A few patterns come up over and over in this area.

**The tub is dead space.** In most homes, if there is a second full bath with a tub, the
tub gets used for storage. Trading it for a larger shower makes the room feel bigger
without moving a single wall.

**Aging in place is a real plan, not a someday.** Manatee and Sarasota Counties skew older
than the state average, and stepping over a 15-inch tub wall onto a wet surface is the most
dangerous 15 inches in the house. A curbless shower removes that step entirely.

**Resale in a retirement market.** Buyers touring a Lakewood Ranch or Longboat Key home
increasingly look for at least one no-step shower. Keeping one tub somewhere in the house
is smart; keeping three is not.

**Old tub surrounds fail quietly.** Florida humidity is unforgiving, and plenty of the jobs
we open up reveal soft substrate behind the surround from a decade of slow moisture. The
conversion often fixes a problem, not just a look.

## What a conversion actually involves

The tub comes out. That much is obvious. What matters is what replaces it.

A standard alcove tub is roughly 60 inches by 30–32 inches. That footprint becomes your new
shower, and the work runs in this order:

1. **Demolition.** Tub, surround, and the wall board behind it come out down to the studs.
2. **Plumbing changes.** The tub drain sits at one end; a shower drain usually needs to move
   to the center, or be swapped for a linear drain at one wall. The valve gets raised and
   often replaced with a pressure-balanced or thermostatic mixer.
3. **Framing and floor prep.** If you want curbless, the joist bay under the shower usually
   has to be recessed so the shower floor sits flush with the bathroom floor.
4. **Waterproofing.** This is the whole ballgame. We install
   [Schluter waterproofing systems](/services/custom-showers) — Kerdi board and membrane —
   and flood-test the assembly before a single tile goes on.
5. **Tile.** Walls, floor, niches, bench, and any pattern work.
6. **Glass, fixtures, and walkthrough.** Frameless panel or door, trim, grout, seal, and a
   final review.

For most single-shower projects, that runs about 1–2 weeks depending on size, waterproofing,
and tile complexity. A full bathroom remodel typically runs 2–4 weeks.

### Curbed vs. curbless: the decision that drives everything

| Factor | Curbed shower | Curbless (zero-entry) shower |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entry | 3–6 inch curb to step over | Flush with the bathroom floor |
| Accessibility | Limited; a trip point | Walker- and wheelchair-friendly |
| Floor prep | Fits most existing framing | Usually requires recessing the subfloor or a sloped mud bed |
| Drain | Center point drain typical | Linear drain at wall or entry, single-plane slope |
| Waterproofing demands | High | Higher — the bathroom floor is part of the system |
| Look | Traditional | Open, larger-feeling, modern |
| Cost | Lower | Adds labor and framing work |
| Best for | Second and guest baths | Primary baths, aging-in-place, long-term homes |

Curbless isn't just an aesthetic choice on the Gulf Coast — it's the one that keeps the
bathroom usable if mobility changes. If you plan to stay in the house, it's worth the extra
framing. For a guest bath on a tight budget, a low curb with a beveled edge is a reasonable
compromise.

## What a tub to shower conversion costs

Every bathroom is different, so treat these as ranges rather than quotes. Nationally,
[Angi's 2026 data](https://www.angi.com/articles/convert-tub-to-shower-cost.htm) puts
tub-to-shower conversions between **$1,200 and $15,000**, with a custom tiled walk-in shower
landing in the **$3,500 to $15,000** band and prefab stall conversions running
**$1,000 to $8,000**. Barrier-free stall units alone are listed at $2,000 to $4,100 before
installation.

What actually moves the number on a Bradenton-area project:

- **Prefab panel vs. custom tile.** A one-piece acrylic surround is the cheapest path and the
  fastest. [Tile costs more, lasts longer](/services/tile-natural-stone), and is the only
  option if you want a specific look or a non-standard size.
- **Curbless.** Recessing the framing and running a linear drain is real carpentry and real
  plumbing.
- **Drain relocation.** Moving the drain more than a few inches — especially on a slab —
  adds meaningful cost.
- **Tile size and pattern.** Large-format porcelain needs flatter substrate and lippage
  control. Herringbone, mosaic floors, and stone all add labor.
- **Glass.** A frameless fixed panel is less than a full frameless door assembly.
- **Extras.** Benches, multiple niches, heated floors, body sprays, and steam all add scope.
- **What's behind the wall.** Rot, out-of-plumb framing, or old galvanized supply lines get
  found on demo day, not on estimate day.

A quick honest note: the "one-day" conversion ads you see are almost always acrylic liner
systems. They are fast and they work for some homeowners. They are not the same product as a
tiled, flood-tested, curbless shower, and they should not be priced against one.

> **Worth remembering:** budget by what's behind the tile, not by the tile. Waterproofing,
> slope, and drain work are where a conversion is won or lost — and the cheapest quote is
> usually the one that skipped them.

## ADA dimensions worth knowing (even if you're not building to code)

Most private homes are not legally required to meet ADA standards. But the
[2010 ADA Standards, Section 608](https://www.corada.com/documents/2010ADAStandards/608) are
the best available cheat sheet for what actually works for a person with limited mobility,
and a 60-inch tub footprint is remarkably close to several of them.

**Use this as a design checklist:**

- [ ] **Transfer shower:** 36" × 36" clear inside, with a 36" minimum entry
- [ ] **Standard roll-in shower:** 30" wide × 60" deep minimum, with a 60" wide entry
- [ ] **Alternate roll-in shower:** 36" wide × 60" deep minimum, 36" entry at one end
- [ ] **Threshold:** ½ inch maximum height — beveled, rounded, or vertical
- [ ] **Controls:** no higher than 48 inches above the shower floor; on the entry side, not
      under the spray
- [ ] **Grab bars:** blocking installed in the walls now, even if you skip the bars for now
- [ ] **Seat:** a built-in bench, not a stool that slides
- [ ] **Floor:** small-format tile or textured porcelain — more grout lines means more grip
      when wet

That last point matters more than people expect: a polished 24×48 porcelain floor is a hazard
in a shower. Save large format for the walls.

## Permits in Manatee and Sarasota County

Here's the short version: if the project changes plumbing, electrical, or structure, it needs
a permit.

A tub-to-shower conversion almost always does. Relocating the drain and swapping the valve is
plumbing work, and
[local guidance for Sarasota and Manatee](https://www.loftinplumbing.com/bathroom-remodel-permits-sarasota-manatee)
is clear that any drain, water line, or fixture relocation triggers a plumbing permit. Adding
an exhaust fan, new lighting, or a GFCI circuit brings an electrical permit with it. Purely
cosmetic work — new tile over sound substrate, paint, a vanity swap with no plumbing movement
— generally does not.

One thing worth flagging in 2026: Florida is currently operating under the 8th Edition (2023)
Florida Building Code, with the
[9th Edition (2026) taking effect December 31, 2026](https://www.revolutionflorida.com/blog/florida-building-code-9th-edition).
If your project runs into next year, permitting timing is worth a conversation with your
contractor.

The practical advice: let your general contractor pull the permit and run the licensed trades
under it. Unpermitted bathroom work has a way of surfacing at closing — an expensive surprise
in a market where a lot of homes change hands.

## How to tell a shower built to last

Ask any contractor these five questions. The answers tell you almost everything.

1. **What waterproofing system are you using, and who installs it?** You want a named system
   and an in-house crew, not "we'll use the green board."
2. **Do you flood-test before tiling?** The answer should be yes, every time. A flood test is
   a few hours that prevents a five-figure repair.
3. **How are you handling the slope and the drain?** Single-plane slope to a linear drain, or
   a properly sloped mud bed to a center drain. Both work. Neither happens by accident.
4. **Who does the tile?** Subcontracted crews vary wildly. Certifications matter here —
   Schluter-certified installation and NTCA membership are real credentials, not badges.
5. **What's the warranty, and on what?** Materials and workmanship should both be covered.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a tub to shower conversion take?

Most single-shower conversions run 1–2 weeks, depending on size, waterproofing, and tile
complexity. A full bathroom remodel typically takes 2–4 weeks. Curbless work and drain
relocation add time on the front end, before tile starts.

### Does removing the only bathtub hurt my home's resale value?

Keeping at least one tub in the house is the conventional advice, especially if families with
young children are part of your buyer pool. In a retirement-heavy market like Lakewood Ranch
or Longboat Key, converting a rarely-used primary-bath tub to a no-step shower usually helps
rather than hurts — as long as another tub exists somewhere.

### Can you build a curbless shower on a concrete slab?

Yes, and it's common in this area. On slab construction the approach is different than on a
raised floor — often a recessed slab pour or a carefully engineered sloped assembly with a
linear drain at the entry. It's a design conversation before it's a construction one.

### Do I need a permit to replace a bathtub with a shower in Bradenton?

Almost always yes, because the drain and valve get relocated. Plumbing changes require a
plumbing permit in Manatee and Sarasota County; new circuits, lighting, or exhaust fans
require an electrical permit. Your contractor should pull it.

### What's the difference between a curbless shower and a wet room?

A curbless shower is a defined shower area with a flush entry, usually with glass. A wet room
waterproofs [the entire bathroom floor](/services/flooring) and treats the whole
space as a shower zone. Wet rooms feel dramatic and are excellent for accessibility, but they
demand more waterproofing and more careful drainage planning.

### Is tile or a prefab acrylic surround better for a Florida bathroom?

Both can perform well when installed correctly. Acrylic is faster and cheaper, with fewer
seams to maintain. Tile over a properly installed waterproofing membrane lasts longer, allows
any size or shape, and is the only real option for curbless. In a humid climate, what matters
most is the waterproofing behind whichever surface you choose.
