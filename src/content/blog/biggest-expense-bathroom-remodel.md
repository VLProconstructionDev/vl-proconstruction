Short answer: **labor.** On a typical Gulf Coast bathroom it eats 40–65% of the total, and the
single largest slice of that labor goes into the shower.

That surprises people, because labor is the one line on the estimate you can't go look at in a
showroom. You can hold the tile. You can sit in the tub at the plumbing supply house. You
cannot hold four days of waterproofing, a re-routed drain line, or the tile setter who keeps a
60-inch niche dead level across three walls — and those are the things you are actually paying
for.

Here's where the money really goes, in the order it leaves your account.

## Where a bathroom budget actually goes

| Line item | Share of budget | Typical Gulf Coast range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Labor (all trades)** | **40–65%** | **$6,000 – $22,000** |
| Shower / wet area build | 20–30% | $4,500 – $18,000 |
| Cabinetry + countertop | 10–15% | $1,500 – $6,000 |
| Tile + stone material | 8–15% | $1,200 – $5,500 |
| Plumbing fixtures + valves | 8–12% | $900 – $4,500 |
| Electrical, fan, lighting | 4–8% | $600 – $2,500 |
| Permits + inspections | 1–3% | $250 – $900 |

A full bathroom remodel in Bradenton, Lakewood Ranch, and Sarasota generally lands between
**$14,000 and $35,000**, with a shower-only project running **$9,000 to $22,000**. Nationally
Angi puts the average bathroom remodel around $12,000; coastal Florida runs higher because of
what's behind the wall — more on that below.

Notice what isn't at the top of that table. The faucet everyone agonizes over for two weeks is
2% of the job.

## Why labor is the number one expense

A bathroom is the most trade-dense room in a house. It's also the smallest. You are putting
six trades through 40 square feet, in sequence, where each one has to wait on the last.

A single 8×6 bathroom needs, in order: demolition, framing repair, rough plumbing, rough
electrical, inspection, backer board, waterproofing, a flood test, floor tile, wall tile,
grout, drywall and paint, trim, finish plumbing, finish electrical, glass templating, glass
install, and a punch walk. That's 18 steps, most of them a separate visit, several of them a
separate licensed trade, and two of them scheduled around a county inspector.

Nobody is standing around. But somebody has to show up, set up, and clean up seventeen times in
a room the size of a parking space — and that overhead is the expense.

**The rule of thumb:** on a mid-range bathroom, expect roughly $1.50–$2.00 of labor for every
$1.00 of material. Estimates that invert that ratio are usually thin somewhere, and it's rarely
somewhere you can see.

## The single biggest line item: the shower

If labor is the biggest category, the shower is the biggest thing inside it.

A tiled walk-in shower is not a fixture you install — it's an assembly you build. Pan slope,
waterproofing membrane, corner and seam treatment, a flood test held overnight, then tile set
to a plane tight enough that frameless glass will actually sit on it. It's four to six days of
skilled work before anyone sees a finished surface.

| Shower type | Typical installed cost |
| --- | --- |
| Acrylic / fiberglass insert | $3,000 – $6,000 |
| Standard tiled shower, curbed | $7,000 – $13,000 |
| Curbless / zero-entry, tiled | $11,000 – $20,000 |
| Full stone or slab-clad | $16,000 – $30,000+ |

The jump from an insert to a tiled shower is where most bathroom budgets get decided. And the
jump from curbed to curbless is where they get decided a second time — on a Florida slab, a
zero-entry shower means either a recessed pour or an engineered sloped assembly with a linear
drain, and that has to be designed in from day one rather than added in week two.

It's also the part of the job that punishes cheap work hardest. A [failed shower
waterproofing](/blog/bathroom-remodel-mistakes) costs $8,000–$25,000 to put right, because
fixing it means demolishing everything built on top of it.

## The three expenses that blow up Gulf Coast budgets

Two bathrooms with identical finishes can be $10,000 apart. It's almost always one of these.

**1. Moving plumbing.** Leaving the toilet, tub, and vanity where they are is the cheapest
bathroom you will ever build. Move any of them and you're into the slab — and in Manatee and
Sarasota County, most homes are slab-on-grade, which means cutting concrete, re-routing drain
line at the right fall, re-pouring, and an inspection. Budget **$2,000–$6,000** per relocated
drain. A layout change that looks free on paper is the most expensive pencil stroke in
remodeling.

**2. What's behind the wall.** Coastal humidity, older cast iron or polybutylene supply lines,
and decades of small leaks mean demolition regularly reveals soft subfloor, corroded pipe, or
framing that has to be sistered. This is the reason we tell every client to hold **10–15%
contingency**. On a house built before 1990, make it 15%.

**3. Frameless glass.** It gets left off a lot of estimates and it is not a small number:
**$1,200–$3,500** for a typical enclosure, templated only after the tile is finished, then
2–3 weeks out at the fabricator. It's the last thing installed and the one most likely to be
the surprise at the end.

## What actually saves money (and what doesn't)

The instinct is to save on material. It's the wrong lever — material is a third of the job, and
the cheap version of it is the part you touch every day for fifteen years.

**Real savings:**

- **Keep the layout.** The largest single savings available to you, worth $2,000–$6,000, and it costs you nothing visible.
- **Porcelain that reads as stone.** Large-format porcelain gets you a marble look at $4–$9/sq ft instead of $15–$30, with none of the sealing.
- **Standard slab sizes.** A 60-inch vanity off the shelf versus a custom 63-inch is a four-figure decision.
- **One tile, two places.** Running the same tile on the floor and shower walls cuts waste, trim pieces, and setting time.
- **Book the off-season.** January through March is peak season here. Summer scheduling is easier and pricing is softer.

**False savings:**

- **Thin waterproofing.** The one invisible item you cannot inspect and cannot cheaply redo.
- **Skipping the permit.** Fines, forced demo, and a problem at resale — see [the seven mistakes](/blog/bathroom-remodel-mistakes).
- **Buying your own material to "save the markup."** Short orders and dye-lot mismatches stall a job for weeks, and nobody warranties a tile they didn't supply.
- **The lowest bid per square foot.** It almost always describes a smaller job than the other two bids, not a cheaper one.

## FAQs

**What is the most expensive part of a bathroom remodel?**
Labor — 40–65% of the total — and within that, building the shower. A tiled shower is four to
six days of skilled work before any finished surface is visible.

**How much does a bathroom remodel cost in Bradenton or Sarasota?**
A full bathroom typically runs $14,000–$35,000; a shower-only project runs $9,000–$22,000. The
spread is driven by whether the plumbing moves and whether the shower is curbed or curbless.

**Is it cheaper to remodel a bathroom or just replace the shower?**
A [tub-to-shower conversion](/blog/tub-to-shower-conversion-bradenton) in the existing footprint
is far cheaper than a full remodel and delivers most of the daily improvement. It's the highest
return per dollar in the room.

**How much should I set aside for surprises?**
10–15% of the total. On a pre-1990 slab home on the Gulf Coast, use 15% — the subfloor and
supply lines are the two most common finds.

**Does a bathroom remodel add value to a Florida home?**
A mid-range bathroom remodel typically returns 60–70% of its cost at resale, and dated
bathrooms are one of the fastest ways a listing stalls. The return is better on the first
bathroom than the third.

## Get a number you can actually plan around

The useful estimate isn't the lowest one — it's the one that names the shower assembly, the
waterproofing system, whether plumbing moves, and who's pulling the permit. Those four lines
explain nearly every dollar of difference between bids.
