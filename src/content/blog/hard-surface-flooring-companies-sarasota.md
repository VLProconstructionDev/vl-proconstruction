## Why Sarasota is its own flooring problem

Most flooring advice online is written for a house with a basement in Ohio. Almost none of
it applies here, and the difference is not cosmetic.

**Nearly everything sits on a concrete slab.** Slab-on-grade construction means your floor
lives on top of a big, permanently damp mass of concrete. Moisture moves up through it.
Any hard surface floor installed over a slab that wasn't tested — with a calcium chloride
or in-situ relative humidity test, not a plastic-sheet guess — is a warranty claim waiting
to happen.

**Humidity swings, not temperature swings.** Sarasota runs 60–70% relative humidity most
of the year, then a house gets closed up and air-conditioned to 45% for a season while the
owners are north. Wood moves with moisture, not heat. That seasonal cycle is what cups
boards and opens gaps, and it's why acclimation and expansion gaps matter more here than
almost anywhere.

**Salt air on the keys.** On Siesta Key, Lido, and Longboat, fasteners corrode, thresholds
degrade faster, and anything ferrous in a wet area has a short life.

**Post-storm moisture cycles.** After a hurricane season, a lot of slabs in this market
have taken on water that hasn't finished leaving. Installing over one of those on the
original schedule is how you get a floor that delaminates.

A flooring company that can't talk you through those four things fluently is a company
that will find out about them on your job.

## How we ranked these companies

Five criteria, weighted toward the things that actually determine whether a floor lasts:

1. **Who does the work.** In-house crews, owner-supervised, or day-labor subcontractors
   pulled off a list? This is the single biggest predictor of quality.
2. **Subfloor prep as a line item.** If moisture testing, grinding, and self-levelling
   aren't in the written estimate, they're going to become change orders — or get skipped.
3. **Written workmanship warranty.** Material warranties come from the manufacturer and
   are worth little if the install voided them. The installer's own warranty is the one
   that matters.
4. **Licensed and insured, verifiable.** Ask for the certificate, not the claim.
5. **Itemized pricing.** A single lump-sum number is a company hiding where the money
   goes.

## Retailer, franchise, or specialty contractor?

Those five companies represent three different business models, and the right one depends
less on quality than on what you need.

**A showroom retailer** (6051 Design Source) is the right first stop when the product
decision is unsettled. You get selection and product expertise. You're generally paying a
material markup for it, and installation may be handled by crews the store schedules
rather than employs.

**A franchise installer** (Footprints Floors) gives you a national process and a low-
overhead price on labor, with the flexibility to source material yourself. Good when you
know exactly what you want.

**A specialty contractor** (VL Pro Construction, Sarasota Flooring Company, Flooring 941)
sells labor and craft. This is the model to pick when the job isn't just flooring — when
there's a slab that needs grinding, a bathroom in the same scope, stairs, or a curbless
shower where the floor and the wet area have to meet perfectly. See our full
[services](/services) if that's your situation, or our writeup on
[tub-to-shower conversions](/blog/tub-to-shower-conversion-bradenton) for what that kind of
overlap actually involves.

## Seven questions to ask before you sign anything

Ask all seven, of whoever you're leaning toward. The answers sort the field faster than
any review page.

1. **Will you moisture-test my slab, and will the reading be in writing?** The only
   acceptable answer is yes, with a number.
2. **Who is physically installing this — your employees, or subcontractors?** Then: will
   the same crew be here the whole job?
3. **Is subfloor prep in this estimate or extra?** Grinding and self-levelling on an
   out-of-flat Florida slab is real money. Find out now, not on day two.
4. **What's your written workmanship warranty, in years?** Get the document.
5. **Can I see your license and a current certificate of insurance?** Ask for the
   certificate itself.
6. **How long will the material acclimate on site, and where?** "We deliver it the morning
   of" is a red flag for solid or engineered wood.
7. **Show me an itemized estimate.** Demolition and haul-away, prep, membrane, material,
   labor, trim, transitions — each on its own line.

## FAQs

**Is engineered hardwood or solid hardwood better in Sarasota?**
Engineered, in almost every case. It's real wood on a dimensionally stable core, so it
handles the humidity cycle far better than solid over a slab — and it can be glued
directly down. Solid hardwood over slab-on-grade needs a plywood sleeper system, which
adds cost and height.

**Can I install new flooring over existing tile?**
Sometimes. It comes down to total finished height, substrate integrity, and moisture. It
has to be evaluated on site — if someone answers this over the phone without seeing it,
be skeptical.

**Do I need a permit for flooring in Sarasota County?**
Straight floor covering replacement generally doesn't require one. It changes if you're
altering structure, moving plumbing, or working in a condo — most condo associations have
their own rules, including sound-transmission (IIC/STC) minimums for hard surface floors
above the ground floor. Check your association documents before you buy material.

**How long does a flooring install take?**
For a typical 1,200–1,800 sq ft Sarasota home: 3–5 days for LVP, 4–7 for engineered
hardwood, longer if the slab needs significant levelling or the material is still
acclimating. Add days for stairs.

**What's the biggest mistake homeowners make?**
Choosing on price per square foot alone. The cheapest quote is almost always the one that
left prep out — and prep is what determines whether the floor is flat, quiet, and still
tight in five years.
